Direct EvidenceEvidence directly observed, witnessed, or interacted with by the assessor in real-time: [1] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=duELyvjqnBw)Observation of performance: Watching a student perform tasks in a real or simulated workplace.Demonstrations and presentations: Practical display of specific operational or technical skills.Oral questioning: Direct professional conversations, interviews, or viva voce exams testing underpinning knowledge.Role-plays and challenge tests: Simulated workplace scenarios or problem-solving tests. [1] (https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/fact-sheet-assessment-and-assessment-judgement), [2] (https://cloudassess.com/blog/other-parties-collecting-assessment-evidence/), [3] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=duELyvjqnBw)Indirect EvidenceTangible outputs or submissions completed by the student and later reviewed by the assessor: [1] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=duELyvjqnBw)Work samples and portfolios: Collected artifacts, project work, plans, or documents produced over time.Written assignments and projects: Essays, reports, or workplace documentation.Tests, quizzes, and exams: Written or digital knowledge assessments. [1] (https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/fact-sheet-assessment-and-assessment-judgement), [2] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=duELyvjqnBw)Supplementary EvidenceEvidence gathered by third parties (such as workplace supervisors or employers) when the assessor cannot be directly present: [1] (https://legacy.asqa.gov.au/resources/guides/guide-using-other-parties-collect-assessment-evidence)Supervisor/third-party reports: Structured observation checklists or performance logs completed by qualified workplace mentors.Employer testimonials and references: Statements confirming consistent workplace performance and competency.Logbooks: Signed records of hours or tasks completed on the job. [1] (https://www.rtopilot.com.au/article/asqa-rules-of-evidence-implementation), [2] (https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/fact-sheet-assessment-and-assessment-judgement)All collected evidence must satisfy ASQA’s Rules of Evidence—meaning it must be valid (aligned with the unit), sufficient (enough quantity/quality), authentic (the student's own work), and current (recent enough to reflect up-to-date abilities). You can read more via the ASQA Assessment Practice Guide.
